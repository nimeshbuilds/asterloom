THE POCKET GARDEN EXPLORERS
Original fictional learning material for Asterloom • NimeshBuilds

A garden of made-up plants

The Pocket Garden has three planting beds and four kinds of pretend seeds. Your job is to read the seed cards and give every seed a suitable home. These plants and their needs are invented. This is a sorting and reasoning exercise, not advice about growing real plants. It is written for readers around ages 8 to 10, with help from an adult when useful.

Meet the three beds

Sunny Bed has bright light and dry soil. Puddle Bed has bright light and wet soil. Shade Bed has low light and wet soil. A bed can hold several seeds. There is no bed with both low light and dry soil.

Each seed card gives two needs: light and soil. Both needs must match the bed. A pretty name or a favorite color cannot tell you what a seed needs. Read its card instead.

Meet the seeds

A Glowbud needs bright light and dry soil. A Splashleaf needs bright light and wet soil. A Mooncurl needs low light and wet soil. A Duskdot needs low light and dry soil. Those needs stay the same throughout this exercise.

Try a careful match

Glowbud needs bright light. Sunny Bed and Puddle Bed both have that light, so either one passes the first check. But Glowbud also needs dry soil. Puddle Bed is wet, so it fails the second check. Sunny Bed matches both needs. Sunny Bed is therefore the right home for Glowbud.

Splashleaf belongs in Puddle Bed because both are bright and wet. Mooncurl belongs in Shade Bed because both are low-light and wet. Duskdot has no matching bed. The careful choice is to leave Duskdot on the waiting shelf until a suitable bed exists. Waiting is better than pretending one of its needs does not matter.

What if something changes?

A garden helper puts a screen over Sunny Bed. While the screen is there, Sunny Bed has low light instead of bright light. Its soil stays dry. Now Duskdot matches Sunny Bed, but Glowbud does not. The change affects one property, not both. Removing the screen restores Sunny Bed's original bright light.

Explain your thinking

You can explain a match with two short reasons: “Mooncurl goes in Shade Bed because it needs low light and wet soil.” If a seed does not fit anywhere, say which combination is missing. If a card is smudged and you cannot read the soil need, ask for a clear card. You do not need to guess to be a good explorer.

The useful skill is checking every rule, noticing what changed, and being able to explain your choice. A mistaken match is a chance to reread the two needs and try a better plan.
