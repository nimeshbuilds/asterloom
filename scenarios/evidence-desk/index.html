First time here? Set up Asterloom +
  1. Install the Mac preview. Download the ZIP from the v0.7.14 release, expand it, and open Asterloom. It supports macOS 13 or later on Apple silicon and Intel. There is no Windows installer in this release.
  2. Connect AI for creation. In a new game, choose Connect AI to create. In Connect your AI, select an available provider, complete its connection flow, select an available model, then choose Back to my game. Provider charges or usage limits may apply.
  3. Keep this guide beside the desktop app. Download the sample source below, or expand its preview to copy it. The website provides instructions and materials; create and play inside Asterloom.

Finished games play without a live AI connection. AI chat with Aster is a separate, optional connection. These guides use the published preview’s controls; its gameplay is older than the development screenshot on the homepage.

AI creation time and the chosen activity vary with the provider and source. The sample checks help you inspect the result; they are not a guarantee of a particular game or learning outcome. The samples are original fictional material for personal and family learning under the Asterloom license.

One step at a time

Let’s try it.

Follow these steps in the desktop app. Mark them here as you go.

  1. 01

    Add the exhibit record

    Download evidence-desk.txt. Choose Create a game, then Upload files, and add it. Select Adults and Investigate and explain.

    You’re ready when

    The source contains Blue, Green, and Amber observations plus a section on evidence limits.

  2. 02

    Create and learn

    Connect your chosen AI and model, then choose Create game with AI. When ready, choose Play game and read the lesson before opening the quest.

    You’re ready when

    You can distinguish the number who followed from the proportion of stopped visitors who followed.

  3. 03

    Ask regular Aster

    Open Aster. If she is already connected to AI, choose Use regular Aster. Ask: What does the record say about Green's follow-through proportion? Read the source passages she finds.

    You’re ready when

    The reference says eight of ten stopped visitors followed: 80 percent.

  4. 04

    Check her current context

    Open Current activity & tools in Aster and choose What is next? Compare her response with the lesson or mission currently on screen.

    You’re ready when

    The guidance describes your current activity and available next step, rather than a new unrelated assignment.

  5. 05

    Try an optional AI explanation

    Choose Connect Aster to AI, confirm a provider and model, then Enable AI for Aster. Ask: Why can Green have the highest follow-through proportion while Blue and Amber have more followers? Read the explanation and its cited material.

    You’re ready when

    The explanation distinguishes Green's 8/10 proportion from Blue's and Amber's nine followers.

  6. 06

    Ask about an unknown

    Ask: What did it cost to print the Green sign? Check the Limits of the evidence section in the source. The record contains no printing costs, so a supported answer must not invent a price.

    You’re ready when

    You can identify a question that this source cannot answer.

  7. 07

    Keep the evidence

    In Aster, open Current activity & tools and choose Download sources. Select the readable text to save a copy. Use the source checks below to evaluate any generated explanation.

    You’re ready when

    Your downloaded reference includes the measurements and their limitations.

The payoff

What a good result looks like.

  • You find relevant passages using regular Aster without requesting a live AI explanation.
  • You can compare an optional AI answer with its source evidence.
  • You recognize that missing printing costs and unmeasured learning cannot be inferred from the observations.

Regular Aster retrieves passages and provides prepared or contextual help; it does not generate the same explanations as connected AI. AI answers can still be wrong. Creation AI and AI Aster are separate opt-ins, and AI chat uses the selected provider's limits or credits.

Make the learning visible

Can you explain it?

Try answering from memory, then open the source-based answer to compare.

Which sign had the highest proportion of stopped visitors who followed the arrow?

Green: 8 of 10 stopped visitors, or 80 percent. Blue had 75 percent and Amber had 60 percent.

Which sign had the largest number of followers, and does that establish that its color caused more learning?

Blue and Amber tied at 9 followers. This does not establish a learning effect: learning was not measured, the signs appeared at different times, and comparable conditions were not established.

How much did the Green sign cost to print?

The source does not say. Printing costs are explicitly listed as missing information, so no amount can be supported from this record.

THE NORTH WINDOW EXHIBIT RECORD
Original fictional learning material for Asterloom • NimeshBuilds

What this record can tell us

North Window Gallery tested three signs for the same fictional exhibit. This record contains the observations from one afternoon. It teaches the difference between reporting a measurement, comparing measurements, and claiming a cause. It does not describe a real study or make a general claim about how all people behave.

The three observations

The Blue sign was used from 1:00 to 1:30. Twelve visitors stopped to read it. Of those twelve, nine followed the arrow to the exhibit. The Green sign was used from 1:30 to 2:00. Ten visitors stopped to read it, and eight of them followed the arrow. The Amber sign was used from 2:00 to 2:30. Fifteen visitors stopped to read it, and nine of them followed the arrow.

“Stopped” means the visitor paused in front of the sign for at least three seconds. “Followed” means that a stopped visitor then entered the exhibit corridor. Staff counted each person only once within a sign's half-hour period. The record contains no measurement of whether a visitor enjoyed the exhibit or remembered its content.

Counts and proportions answer different questions

Blue and Amber each had nine followers, so they tied for the largest number of followers. Green had eight followers. To compare the proportion of stopped visitors who followed, divide followers by stopped visitors. Blue's proportion is 9 divided by 12, or 75 percent. Green's is 8 divided by 10, or 80 percent. Amber's is 9 divided by 15, or 60 percent.

A worked interpretation

“Green had the highest follow-through proportion among the visitors who stopped” is supported by these measurements. “Green attracted the most readers” is not: Amber had fifteen stopped visitors, more than either other sign. “Green caused visitors to learn more” is also unsupported, because learning was not measured.

Limits of the evidence

The signs appeared at different times. The record does not say whether visitor groups, corridor traffic, or other conditions were comparable. These observations therefore do not establish that sign color caused the differences. They describe what happened in these three periods.

The record does not include the signs' wording, printing costs, visitor ages, or results from other days. A question about any of those details cannot be answered from this source. An honest response names the missing detail instead of inventing it. More confident wording does not replace missing evidence.

A useful comparison states the measure and its scope: “Green: 80 percent of stopped visitors in its observed half hour.” This is more precise than “Green was best.” Before recommending a sign for another setting, collect comparable observations and decide what outcome matters: stopping, following, enjoyment, or learning.

If you get stuck.

I used AI to create the game, but Aster only shows passages.

Open Aster, choose Connect Aster to AI, then Enable AI for Aster. Her live AI connection is enabled separately and lasts for this app session.

Aster supplies a printing price or a claim about learning.

Do not accept that as source evidence. The source explicitly omits printing costs and does not measure learning. Use Download sources to verify the limitation and report the unsupported answer.
