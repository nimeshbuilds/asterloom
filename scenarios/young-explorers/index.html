First time here? Set up Asterloom +
  1. Install the Mac preview. Download the ZIP from the v0.7.14 release, expand it, and open Asterloom. It supports macOS 13 or later on Apple silicon and Intel. There is no Windows installer in this release.
  2. Connect AI for creation. In a new game, choose Connect AI to create. In Connect your AI, select an available provider, complete its connection flow, select an available model, then choose Back to my game. Provider charges or usage limits may apply.
  3. Keep this guide beside the desktop app. Download the sample source below, or expand its preview to copy it. The website provides instructions and materials; create and play inside Asterloom.

Finished games play without a live AI connection. AI chat with Aster is a separate, optional connection. These guides use the published preview’s controls; its gameplay is older than the development screenshot on the homepage.

AI creation time and the chosen activity vary with the provider and source. The sample checks help you inspect the result; they are not a guarantee of a particular game or learning outcome. The samples are original fictional material for personal and family learning under the Asterloom license.

  4. 04

    Learn the two-property rule

    Follow the lesson's idea controls. For Glowbud, compare light first, then soil. Say the two reasons aloud before moving into the guided quest.

    You’re ready when

    The learner explains that Sunny Bed is both bright and dry.

  5. 05

    Treat a mismatch as information

    Try the generated activity and revisit the source when a match fails. Discuss Duskdot: none of the original beds is both low-light and dry, so the waiting shelf is a valid decision.

The payoff

What a good result looks like.

  • The game was created with the chosen age group and the supplied garden material.
  • The learner can name both properties behind a match.
  • The learner can explain a no-match case and reconsider a choice when one rule changes.

The source was written for readers around ages 8–10; Ages 9–11 is the selected app setting for this guide. Age adaptation and generated wording still need adult review. The seeds are fictional, and this is not gardening advice.

Make the learning visible

Can you explain it?

Try answering from memory, then open the source-based answer to compare.

Where does Glowbud belong before any screen is added, and why?

Sunny Bed, because Glowbud needs bright light and dry soil, and Sunny Bed has both.

Where should Duskdot go before the screen changes Sunny Bed?

The waiting shelf. Duskdot needs low light and dry soil, and none of the three original beds has that combination.

Which seed fits Sunny Bed while its screen is in place?

Duskdot. The screen changes Sunny Bed to low light while the soil stays dry. Glowbud no longer fits because it needs bright light.

THE POCKET GARDEN EXPLORERS
Original fictional learning material for Asterloom • NimeshBuilds

A garden of made-up plants

The Pocket Garden has three planting beds and four kinds of pretend seeds. Your job is to read the seed cards and give every seed a suitable home. These plants and their needs are invented. This is a sorting and reasoning exercise, not advice about growing real plants. It is written for readers around ages 8 to 10, with help from an adult when useful.

Meet the three beds

Sunny Bed has bright light and dry soil. Puddle Bed has bright light and wet soil. Shade Bed has low light and wet soil. A bed can hold several seeds. There is no bed with both low light and dry soil.

Each seed card gives two needs: light and soil. Both needs must match the bed. A pretty name or a favorite color cannot tell you what a seed needs. Read its card instead.

Meet the seeds

A Glowbud needs bright light and dry soil. A Splashleaf needs bright light and wet soil. A Mooncurl needs low light and wet soil. A Duskdot needs low light and dry soil. Those needs stay the same throughout this exercise.

Try a careful match

Glowbud needs bright light. Sunny Bed and Puddle Bed both have that light, so either one passes the first check. But Glowbud also needs dry soil. Puddle Bed is wet, so it fails the second check. Sunny Bed matches both needs. Sunny Bed is therefore the right home for Glowbud.

Splashleaf belongs in Puddle Bed because both are bright and wet. Mooncurl belongs in Shade Bed because both are low-light and wet. Duskdot has no matching bed. The careful choice is to leave Duskdot on the waiting shelf until a suitable bed exists. Waiting is better than pretending one of its needs does not matter.

What if something changes?

A garden helper puts a screen over Sunny Bed. While the screen is there, Sunny Bed has low light instead of bright light. Its soil stays dry. Now Duskdot matches Sunny Bed, but Glowbud does not. The change affects one property, not both. Removing the screen restores Sunny Bed's original bright light.

Explain your thinking

You can explain a match with two short reasons: “Mooncurl goes in Shade Bed because it needs low light and wet soil.” If a seed does not fit anywhere, say which combination is missing. If a card is smudged and you cannot read the soil need, ask for a clear card. You do not need to guess to be a good explorer.

The useful skill is checking every rule, noticing what changed, and being able to explain your choice. A mistaken match is a chance to reread the two needs and try a better plan.
