What to look for

Export exactly one game as an editable notebook, import it into another copy of Asterloom, and inspect the source material that travels with it.

First time here? Set up Asterloom +
  1. Install the Mac preview. Download the ZIP from the v0.7.14 release, expand it, and open Asterloom. It supports macOS 13 or later on Apple silicon and Intel. There is no Windows installer in this release.
  2. Connect AI for creation. In a new game, choose Connect AI to create. In Connect your AI, select an available provider, complete its connection flow, select an available model, then choose Back to my game. Provider charges or usage limits may apply.
  3. Keep this guide beside the desktop app. Download the sample source below, or expand its preview to copy it. The website provides instructions and materials; create and play inside Asterloom.

Finished games play without a live AI connection. AI chat with Aster is a separate, optional connection. These guides use the published preview’s controls; its gameplay is older than the development screenshot on the homepage.

AI creation time and the chosen activity vary with the provider and source. The sample checks help you inspect the result; they are not a guarantee of a particular game or learning outcome. The samples are original fictional material for personal and family learning under the Asterloom license.

One step at a time

Let’s try it.

Follow these steps in the desktop app. Mark them here as you go.

  1. 01

    Create a game to share

    Download pass-it-on.txt. Use Create a game → Upload files, select Adults and Investigate and explain, then connect AI and choose Create game with AI. Alternatively, reuse a completed game from an earlier guide for the export workflow.

    You’re ready when

    Your selected game is finished and contains only material you are comfortable sharing.

  2. 02

    Review before handing it over

    Choose Play game, read its teaching, and try a mission. For Paper Bridge, compare the lesson with the source's original and revised briefs. Choose Save & return and wait for Saved on this device.

    You’re ready when

    You have checked the generated content, rather than assuming an export makes it correct.

  3. 03

    Export one notebook

    On the game home, choose Export this notebook, then Editable notebook. Save the .learnpack file. This option contains the selected notebook; it is not your entire game library.

    You’re ready when

    A .learnpack file is saved. Your original game remains in Your games.

  4. 04

    Import in another workspace

    Share the file with another personal learner who has Asterloom, or use a separate installation of your own. In the receiving app choose Your games → Import a notebook and select the .learnpack. Open the imported game.

    You’re ready when

    The recipient sees the imported notebook without needing your AI credentials.

  5. 05

    Play and inspect the source

    Choose Play game in the receiving app. Open Source materials and choose Download readable text; Download original file is also available when the source was uploaded. No live AI is needed for ordinary play.

    You’re ready when

    The learning material is present inside the imported game and can be saved separately.

  6. 06

    Make a separate remix

    Return to the game home, open More options, and choose Make a remix. If you want to change its sources or learning preferences, choose Add sources & recreate in the editor, then build the new edition with your own AI connection.

    You’re ready when

    The original imported game stays available. Editing source-based content requires a separate edition and a new creation run.

  7. 07

    Compare the two briefs

    For the Paper Bridge kit, use the checks below to compare the original 12-centimeter gap with the revised 18-centimeter gap. For another reused game, compare its own source rules instead.

    You’re ready when

    Your explanation preserves fixed catalog facts and identifies which requirements changed.

The payoff

What a good result looks like.

  • A .learnpack transfers one selected notebook, not the sender's whole library.
  • The recipient can play and inspect bundled material without receiving provider credentials or the sender's personal progress.
  • A remix or new edition remains separate from the original game.

A .learnpack needs Asterloom to open; it is not a standalone app. The separate Mac app · Universal export requires an available Mac runtime. Windows export requires its Windows runtime. Exported files include their bundled sources and retain applicable licenses; personal progress and provider credentials are excluded.

THE PAPER BRIDGE DESIGN LAB
Original fictional learning material for Asterloom • NimeshBuilds

A design another learner can understand

Paper Bridge is a fictional planning lab. You choose a bridge design for a small model display, explain the choice, and leave enough information for someone else to explore a different requirement. The capacities below are invented properties of named model pieces. They are not engineering claims or instructions for a real bridge.

The design catalog

Flat Deck spans 10 centimeters, supports 2 counters, and costs 2 credits. Folded Deck spans 15 centimeters, supports 4 counters, and costs 4 credits. Arch Deck spans 20 centimeters, supports 5 counters, and costs 6 credits. Each design can span a shorter gap than its listed maximum, and each can carry fewer counters than its capacity. You must choose one complete design; designs cannot be joined together or partly purchased.

The original brief

The display has a gap of 12 centimeters. The bridge must support 3 counters. The budget is 5 credits. A valid design must satisfy all three constraints: enough span, enough capacity, and a cost within budget. If multiple designs are valid, choose the cheapest. If no design is valid, report which constraints prevent a solution instead of changing the catalog.

A worked selection

Flat Deck costs only 2 credits, but its 10-centimeter span cannot cross the 12-centimeter gap and its 2-counter capacity is too low. Folded Deck spans 15 centimeters and supports 4 counters, so it meets both requirements. Its cost of 4 credits fits the 5-credit budget. Arch Deck meets the span and capacity requirements but costs 6 credits, exceeding the budget. Folded Deck is the only valid choice for the original brief, leaving 1 credit unspent.

A different brief

Suppose another learner changes the gap to 18 centimeters, keeps the 3-counter requirement, and raises the budget to 6 credits. The catalog stays unchanged. Folded Deck is now too short. Arch Deck spans 20 centimeters, supports 5 counters, and fits the new budget exactly. Arch Deck is the valid choice.

If the new budget stayed at 5 credits instead, no listed design would work: the two cheaper designs would be too short, while Arch Deck would be too expensive. That is a meaningful result, not a failure to pick a favorite design.

Explain a revision clearly

A useful handoff separates fixed facts from changed requirements. State the catalog, the new gap, the load, and the budget. Then compare every candidate against every constraint. Keep the original brief alongside the revised one so another person can see why the conclusion changed. A shared answer becomes a reusable lesson when its evidence and assumptions travel with it.

If you get stuck.

Can someone double-click the .learnpack without Asterloom?

No. Use Import a notebook in Asterloom for this guide. For a standalone desktop bundle, use an available platform option in Export this notebook and keep the complete exported application together.

My game contains large recordings and Editable notebook is unavailable.

Use Notebook with all recordings to save a .learnzip archive. The receiving desktop app uses Import recording archive. This small text-only kit does not need that option.

Why is the recipient's progress different from mine?

Exports exclude your personal learning progress. Each learner starts and saves their own progress; your original results stay in your local game.
